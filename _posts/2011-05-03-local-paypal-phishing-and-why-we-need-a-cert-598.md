This just came in the mail: (twice - at two different mailboxes - I must be a high value target for these guys)

<a href="http://www.iamit.org/assets/uploads/2011/05/phishmail.png"><img class="size-large wp-image-599 alignnone" title="phishmail" src="http://www.iamit.org/assets/uploads/2011/05/phishmail-466x700.png" alt="" width="466" height="700" /></a>

A classic phishing email, with the only exception that it seems highly targeted at the Israeli market! (yeah - I know, I sound a little excited, but this is the first one I ever got...). Obviously, I am not the new owner of a BROWN denim jeans (eeewww!), so as I am very interested in who may want my PayPal details, a bit of digging brought this up:

&nbsp;
<ol>
	<li>The phishing site (the one led to by the obvious "CANCEL TRANSACTION" link) is hosted on al3abnt.com.</li>
	<li>al3abnt.com is obviously not related to PayPal, and in a very unusual turn of events it is actually registered to a person, or at least something that may lead closer to a person than most phishing sites (that use whois anonymizing).</li>
	<li>The Whois registration (see below) also leads to a website on anasblog.me. This seems a personal blog from a local village called Salfit in Israel (I knew it reminded me of something... been around there a couple of times :-)).</li>
<a href="http://www.iamit.org/assets/uploads/2011/05/whois.png"><img class="alignnone size-full wp-image-600" title="whois" src="http://www.iamit.org/assets/uploads/2011/05/whois.png" alt="" width="299" height="309" /></a>

&nbsp;
	<li>The blog (see screenshot below) seems pretty anti-Israeli (note the "we are with the third intifada" button on the top-left corner) - thus explaining the interest in local Israeli PayPal accounts.</li>
<a href="http://www.iamit.org/assets/uploads/2011/05/arabicblog.png"><img class="alignnone size-large wp-image-601" title="arabicblog" src="http://www.iamit.org/assets/uploads/2011/05/arabicblog-700x525.png" alt="" width="620" height="465" /></a>
	<li>Obviously - there's no-one to send the notification to... no CERT would handle this, and the police is almost comical in the way they reacted to calls of this nature...</li>
</ol>
I'm guessing that a CERT would have done the following:
<ol>
	<li>Publish a warning notification on the offending site, and the email template.</li>
	<li>Coordinate with ISP the takedown of the offending site and law-enforcement work to apprehend the scammer (A phone number is listed on the whois information - feel free to try it out :-) ).</li>
</ol>
Be safe out there!
