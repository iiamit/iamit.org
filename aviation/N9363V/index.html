01 · The offer

What you're buying.

Half an airplane, a partner who flies it well and maintains it properly, and a hangar spot 20 minutes from Manhattan.

N9363V is owned by a corporation. You buy 50% of the shares in that corporation, and from that point the two of us own and run the airplane together as equal partners. No club, no board, no committee. Two owners, one airplane, one shared calendar.

I've owned and flown this airplane since 2022 and put roughly 400 hours on it. It gets flown regularly, which is the single best thing that happens to a 1970 airframe. It lives in a hangar, it goes to the same shop every year, and the logs are complete and consecutive back to new.

I'm a CFI/CFII with 800+ hours, most of them in Mooneys. If you're new to the type, transition training comes with the deal (see the aviation page for what I do). You'll need it for insurance anyway, and I'd rather teach you the airplane myself than hand you the keys and hope.

Who this suits

A good fit: an instrument-rated pilot in the NY/NJ metro who wants a fast, efficient traveling airplane and flies 75 to 125 hours a year. Someone who wants real IFR capability without a $400,000 entry price.

Not a fit: a pilot who needs the airplane on demand every weekend, wants to base it elsewhere, or is looking for a primary trainer. A retractable complex single is the wrong first airplane.

05 · Partnership

How it works.

A two-person partnership, in plain terms. Nothing here is complicated, and all of it goes in writing before money moves.

01

You buy shares, not a tail number

The aircraft is registered to a corporation. You purchase 50% of that corporation's shares. Title to the airplane never moves, so there's no re-registration and no new bill of sale on the airframe itself.

02

Equal owners, equal say

Two 50% partners. Routine maintenance, scheduling, and consumables run on agreement. Anything discretionary above an agreed dollar threshold, upgrades and major work included, needs both signatures.

03

Fixed costs split down the middle

Hangar, insurance, annual inspection, database subscriptions, and the corporation's own filing costs are shared 50/50 through a joint operating account funded by monthly contributions.

04

You pay for what you fly

Variable costs, meaning fuel, oil, and an hourly reserve for the engine and prop, are paid by whoever flew the hours. Tach time is logged on a shared sheet and settled monthly.

05

Shared calendar, no minimums

Scheduling is first come, first served on a shared online calendar, with a fair-use rule for holidays and long trips. No monthly minimum hours and no block-time buy-in.

06

A clean way out

The co-ownership agreement includes a right of first refusal and an agreed process for valuing and selling a share, so either partner can exit without stranding the other or forcing a sale of the airplane.

Disclosure

Damage history: a gear-up landing and light hail damage, both prior to my purchase in 2022, both repaired and documented in the logs. Nothing has been hidden and nothing is undocumented.

The buyer is welcome to a pre-buy inspection at a shop of their choosing, at their expense. I'll make the airplane and the complete logs available for it. I'd think less of a buyer who didn't ask.

Purchase of a share is subject to a written co-ownership agreement and to the insurer's approval of the incoming pilot. Both parties should have their own counsel review the share transfer and the operating agreement.
